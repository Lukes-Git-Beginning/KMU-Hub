## 0) What the reference image is doing “right” (your north star)
Key qualities to replicate consistently across all themes:

- **Composition & proportions**
  - Big, *wide* window opening that dominates the center.
  - **Thin, modern frame** with soft bevels and subtle AO (ambient occlusion) where frame meets wall.
  - A **clear desk/ledge band** at the bottom that reads as a surface (thickness + shadow), not just a flat strip.
  - Slight “room context” around the window (wall area) but not so much that the window feels like a picture frame.

- **Material rendering**
  - Wall has **plaster micro-variation** (soft mottling, subtle roughness, gentle gradients).
  - Desk has **believable wood grain + specular response** (directional highlight, micro-scratches).
  - Edges have **contact shadows** and soft bounce light.

- **Atmosphere**
  - Light feels *motivated* (sun direction), with gentle bloom and haze.
  - View outside has depth: foreground/midground/background separation.

Use these as hard requirements in every prompt.

## 2) Common issues across all images (patterns of failure)

### 2.1 Window/frame problems
- **Frames are consistently too thick and too “picture frame.”**
- Many have **multiple nested borders** (especially Raumstation) that create visual clutter around the UI overlay.
- The reference reads as a **thin architectural frame + subtle wall reveal**, not a decorative frame.

**Actionable target**
- At 1536×1024, aim for a frame that *reads* like **8–12 px** on the final delivered image.
- Use: thin bezel, small radius corners, subtle bevel, soft AO.

### 2.2 Flat/low-detail textures
- Walls: often one noise layer → looks like “AI texture,” not plaster.
- Desks: wood grain exists but lacks **micro-contrast** and **specular breakup**.
- Brick/metal: too uniform; missing edge wear, mortar variation, seam AO, anisotropy.

**Actionable target**
- Prompt for: “micro-texture, material study, subtle imperfections, edge wear, contact shadows, ambient occlusion.”

### 2.3 Proportion/composition inconsistency
- Window sizes vary; some are tall, some narrow.
- Desk band height varies; sometimes it’s a thin strip, sometimes a huge table.
- Wall margins vary; reference has balanced margins and a stable “template” feel.

**Actionable target (template rules)**
- Window opening: ~**78–84% of image width**, ~**62–70% of image height**.
- Desk/ledge: ~**14–18%** of image height.
- Wall above window: enough to breathe (~8–12% height), but not huge.

### 2.4 Lighting quality
- Most scenes have **even, flat lighting** on the wall and frame.
- Missing: soft gradients, bounce light, subtle bloom, volumetric cues (where appropriate).

**Actionable target**
- “soft global illumination, gentle bloom, atmospheric perspective, volumetric sun rays (only where motivated), rim light on frame.”

### 2.5 Theme immersion
- Several themes are essentially “same room, different outside image.”
- Since walls/desk must be empty, immersion must come from:
  - **Material language** (brick vs plaster vs metal vs timber)
  - **Lighting color + bounce**
  - **Architectural detailing** (still minimal, but frame reveal, sill material, panel seams)

### 2.6 Window dividers / muntins risk
- You correctly forbid muntins/crossbars. Keep repeating this in prompts.
- Also forbid “pane divisions,” “window grid,” “French window,” “sash window.”

### 2.7 Empty wall/desk compliance
- You’re already enforcing “no objects,” but GPT models love adding decor.
- You need strong negative constraints and also **positive alternatives**: “clean empty surfaces, uninterrupted wall, no props.”

## 3) GPT‑Image‑1.5 prompt engineering recommendations (actionable)

### 3.1 Prompt structure that tends to work best
Use a **hierarchical, front-loaded spec**:

1) **Composition + layout (hard constraints)**  
2) **Materials (wall, frame, desk) with micro-detail cues**  
3) **Lighting & atmosphere**  
4) **Outside view**  
5) **Style anchor**  
6) **Negative constraints (very explicit)**

GPT‑Image often “locks” composition early; if you mention theme story first, it may invent props/shelves. Start with geometry.

### 3.2 Keywords/phrases that reliably push quality
- “premium illustrated interior background”
- “high-detail material rendering, micro-texture”
- “soft global illumination, ambient occlusion”
- “subtle plaster trowel marks”
- “anisotropic brushed metal highlights”
- “edge wear, tiny imperfections, realistic seams”
- “cinematic atmospheric lighting, gentle bloom”
- “clean minimalist surfaces, no props”

### 3.3 Negative prompting strategies (be redundant)
Use a dedicated “DO NOT” block:
- “no furniture, no chair, no lamp, no shelves, no frames, no posters, no plants, no books, no objects”
- “no window muntins, no crossbars, no grid, no divided panes”
- “no text, no logos, no UI, no people”
- “no picture frame look, not a painting, not a framed artwork”

### 3.4 Optimal length/detail level
- Aim for **120–220 words**. Enough to specify layout + materials + lighting + outside + exclusions.
- Too short → generic. Too long → model may “average” constraints or introduce artifacts.

### 3.5 How to convey “thin window frame” more successfully
Instead of only “thin frame,” specify:
- **Pixel-like intent** and physical analogy:
  - “thin aluminum bezel, minimal modern frame, ~1 cm visible border”
  - “slim profile, delicate edge, subtle bevel, not chunky”
- Also specify what it should *not* be:
  - “not thick wooden picture frame, not ornate molding”

### 3.6 How to get richer textures
Add:
- “material study quality”
- “visible wood pores, fine grain fibers, subtle varnish sheen”
- “plaster depth with gentle mottling and corner vignetting”
- “brick variation: chipped edges, mortar irregularity, slight warping”
- “micro-scratches, fingerprints, subtle grime in seams” (for sci-fi)

### 3.7 How to achieve atmospheric/volumetric lighting
- “volumetric light rays” only when motivated (forest, sunset, dreamy).
- “soft haze, atmospheric perspective outside”
- “bounce light tint from outside onto interior wall and frame”
- “gentle bloom around highlights, filmic tone mapping”

### 3.8 How to make themes truly distinct without adding props
Define each theme by a **materials + lighting + architecture trio**:
- Cozy: white plaster + light oak + cool Nordic daylight
- Dreamy: pale plaster/stone + subtle magical glow + colored bounce
- Nature: timber boards + warm sun shafts + forest haze
- Space: dark panels + cool rim LEDs + subtle reflections
- Atelier: aged brick + warm sunset bounce + dusty wood sill
